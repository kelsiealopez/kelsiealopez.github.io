My research focuses on how genomic variation contributes to the evolution of complex traits in birds. I use comparative genomics, pangenomes, structural variation, and transcriptomic approaches to connect patterns of genome evolution with adaptation and phenotype.

## Dissertation research

### Structural variation and regulatory change in arid-adapted Neotropical birds

My doctoral research investigates the genomic basis of adaptation and convergence in birds inhabiting dry forests and other arid environments.

This work examines evolutionary change at multiple levels of biological organization, including:

- Protein-coding genes
- Structural variants
- Gene regulation
- Gene expression
- Molecular pathways
- Convergent adaptation across distantly related species

A central goal of this work is to understand whether similar environments produce similar genomic solutions, or whether different evolutionary lineages repeatedly arrive at similar phenotypes through distinct molecular mechanisms.

## Pangenomes and structural variation

Bird genomes contain substantial structural variation, including insertions, deletions, duplications, inversions, and other forms of genomic variation that are often missed by traditional reference-based approaches.

My work uses pangenomes and long-read sequencing to investigate:

- Structural variation within and among bird species
- The effects of structural variants on gene content and regulation
- Population-level variation in complex genomic regions
- The relevance of structural variation to conservation genomics
- How pangenomes improve our understanding of avian evolution

## Field and museum-based research

My research is grounded in ornithological fieldwork and natural-history collections. I have participated in and led expeditions in Brazil and the United States, including work with the Museum of Comparative Zoology, the Federal University of Pernambuco, and the Museum of Zoology of the University of São Paulo.

These experiences have included:

- Planning and leading ornithological expeditions
- Collecting blood samples from live birds
- Preparing scientific specimens
- Conducting mist-netting and bird banding
- Collecting genomic and phenotypic data
- Connecting field observations with genomic analyses
